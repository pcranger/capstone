  Why it doesn't seem to detect cars

  I ran your trained model and a standard COCO YOLO model on 22 Australian
  street photos (Sydney, Wollongong, Brisbane, Melbourne, Canberra, Albury,
  Adelaide; zebra and signalised crossings, a roundabout, rain, dusk).

  - It sees cars: 54 vehicles found, against 58 for the COCO model. It is weaker
    on dense, distant traffic (1 car against 13 in a crowded George St photo)
    and missed one bus.
  - It only speaks about looming cars: it warns only when a car's image grows
    fast, meaning it's coming straight at you with contact under 3 seconds. At a
    kerb you face across the road, so traffic moves sideways through the view
    and never triggers a warning. This is the main reason it seems not to detect
    cars. The fix is to announce cars heading toward the crossing line, with
    side, distance and seconds.
  - Car lights read as pedestrian signals: in 3 photos, car traffic lights were
    labelled as pedestrian signals, once as green. The training data has almost
    no car lights seen from the footpath. The fix is to retrain with car lights
    as their own "not a pedestrian signal" class.

  Your questions

  - What YOLO does: it looks at one frozen image and returns boxes, each with a
    label and a confidence. It knows nothing about distance, speed or direction;
    the app adds those by tracking boxes across frames.
  - Training on pedestrian signs when Australia has one design: still needed.
    One design makes the job easier, but the hard part is telling the red/green
    figures from car lights on the same poles, and the model can only learn
    Australian lights from Australian examples. Also, Australian signalised
    crossings have audio-tactile push buttons as standard. So at signals the
    camera mostly helps with alignment, noisy intersections, turning cars, and
    knowing whether a green just started. The bigger unmet need is at crossings
    without signals.
  - Your road/kerb segmentation model (AN-S3): it can't find the kerb. On the
    Adelaide St photo it labelled the whole road as footpath, and it gave a
    usable road/footpath boundary in only 1 of 6 photos. Kerb finding needs a
    new model trained on data with a "curb" class (Mapillary Vistas).

  Your concept, evaluated

  - Keep: Google Maps for the route plus crossing mode only near crossings;
    stopping at the kerb (as a backup to the cane); turning to face the
    crossing; measuring the crossing; car warnings.
  - Change:
    - Where crossings are: Google routes don't say where or what kind of
      crossing you'll use. Add OpenStreetMap data (signal type, island, lanes,
      tactile paving) to announce it ahead.
    - Road width and steps: say them as approximate ("about 14 m, 2 lanes").
      Better still, give progress while crossing ("halfway", "kerb in 3 m")
      using the phone's motion tracking.
    - Scanning: replace free left-to-right scanning with the phone in landscape
      on the 120° ultra-wide lens and a guided "look right, look left".
  - Remove: any "safe to cross" decision. At 50 km/h a two-lane gap needs no car
    within about 100 m. At that distance a car is about 7 px tall in the
    model's input, and the model reliably finds cars only to about 40–60 m. So
    "no car detected" can never mean "clear"; the app warns and you decide.
