Why so much uncertainty
YOLO detects vehicle appearance; it does not classify physical motion. That comes from tracking and camera-compensated optical flow.
The existing classifier requires >=6 coherent points within a vehicle, a valid background affine fit, temporal votes, and at least about 1 second of sustained evidence for stationary. Failures reset histories. The live app additionally forces uncertainty while its walking detector reports walking. Recorded videos have no synchronized IMU; this replay does NOT turn on that walking gate.
INDRA baseline: 360/370 frames had a reliable background fit. Across 5,233 vehicle-track observations: 1,417 moving, 56 stationary, 3,760 uncertain (71.85%). Of uncertain observations, 2,997 (79.7%) had fewer than 6 local features; 130 had no per-track diagnostics. These are observations across time, not independent vehicles or accuracy counts. Therefore simply relaxing global camera fitting is not the main INDRA fix. Distant/small or texture-poor boxes and strict local evidence requirements dominate.
Night adds glare, blur and camera translation/parallax. A single background transform cannot perfectly account for near/far stationary objects when the camera walks.

Experiments
A. Existing app: JSFeat LK, global background affine, temporal flow and box-centre history. Frozen source from current app is read by replay.cjs.
B. Dense local-flow binary: OpenCV DIS backward flow (sign reversed to represent previous-to-current), vehicle interior flow compared with nearby masked background; robust median and temporal majority.
C. Mobile-compatible sparse binary: existing JSFeat flow, nearest 40 background points, median subtraction, relaxed local support (3 points), noise-relative moving threshold, 700 ms temporal votes. Source is candidate-src/tracking/vehicleMotion.ts. All vehicle outputs are MOVING or STATIONARY; supported=false flags insufficient evidence separately. Unsupported/default stationary hypotheses must NOT suppress alerts or establish a clear road.
D. Night regression improvement: dense local affine fit, iteratively reweighted least squares to compensate nearby camera rotation/translation rather than just subtracting a median. Still insufficient.

Recommended next implementation work
Do not adopt a binary classifier until its stationary errors on moving traffic are controlled. Improve temporal association/occlusion handling and texture sampling (including glare edges and small targets), then evaluate longer-baseline camera-compensated tracks. For walking scenes, use synchronized visual-inertial pose and depth/ground geometry rather than assuming one image transform is enough. Keep the classifier's binary hypothesis distinct from evidence validity; a forced label cannot be treated as a measured fact. Need independent full-track annotations, false-positive review, direction accuracy, latency, and stationary-curb/night videos with IMU before live promotion.
